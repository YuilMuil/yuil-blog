# First post of 2026

It's been a while since my last blog post — college has been keeping me busy, and on top of that I've been deep in BoB, so writing just kept getting pushed to the back burner. I'd love to do a proper write-up on my most recent findings, but the bug I'm most proud of still hasn't been assigned a CVE yet, so that'll have to wait a few more months. Stay tuned.

Either way, 2025 was a blast. At the time I was carrying around 21 credit hours per semester, grinding through the 정보보안기사 licensing exam, and simultaneously prepping for BoB — all at once. Somehow, against all odds, I got accepted into BoB *and* passed the written portion of the 정보보안기사 exam. Honestly still a little in disbelief about that one (and I didn't tank my GPA either, so I'll take it).

### Experiences at BoB

BoB was genuinely one of the best experiences I've had. I got to meet a ton of smart, ambitious, hungry, and surprisingly kind people — the kind of crowd that pushes you just by being in the same room. The first phase of education was where I learned the most. Going in, I thought I had a solid foundation from real-world reverse engineering work, but I was humbled pretty quickly.

I walked in thinking I'd hold my own just fine. Then I looked to my left and saw someone who ranked top 3 in CTF competitions nationally. Looked to my right — someone sitting on 20+ CVEs from major vendors. So yeah, that put things in perspective fast.

Also Binary exploitation especially turned out to be way harder than I anticipated. I dove deep into heap manipulation techniques and got a much more granular understanding of why integer overflow bypasses are dangerous in practice — not just theoretically, but on a mechanical level. It was the kind of knowledge you don't pick up from reading writeups alone.

I managed to push through most of the assignments (barely, at times), and somehow ended up as track president for vulnerability analysis, which was a growth experience in its own right — technically and personally.

For the project phase, I leaned into what genuinely interested me and played to my strengths: Windows Kernel Driver bug hunting. I can't go into too much detail, but working on it taught me a lot about real-world workplace dynamics — how actual collaborative research differs from solo hobby hacking, how team momentum works, and where things can go sideways if you're not careful.

I also ended up as the PM, which... was a lesson in humility I didn't know I needed. Being responsible for the team's direction and output gave me a real appreciation for what good leadership actually demands. It's easy to criticize from the outside. Actually doing it is a different story entirely.

Thankfully the project landed well in the end, and a huge part of that was due to our PL and our mentor — who we lovingly nicknamed "god singi" — both of whom I learned an enormous amount from - along with my teammates.

### What's been going on since

Lately I've been heads-down studying — mostly AI, hardware, and math — while continuing to poke at things on the side. Managed to find a few more bugs for MSRC and have started branching into browser vulnerability research, which has been a fun change of pace.
